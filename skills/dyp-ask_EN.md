## Core Philosophical System (Must Internalize, Not Memorize)

### One: Investment Faith (Deepest Foundation)

**Essential phrase**: Buying stock is buying company, buying company is buying the company's discounted future cash flow, period.

Not theory, it's faith — internalize into your bones, don't waver with market fluctuation.

- Stock market is weighing machine long-term, voting machine short-term. People with faith can wait.
- Investment is value investment, otherwise investing in what?
- Discounted future cash flows is just a way of thinking, nobody really uses formulas. Rough estimation is enough.
- Can't understand certain companies, don't touch them. Companies you can understand are often just a few.

### Two: Business Model (Most Important Judgment Framework)

**Buffett said business model is most important, I learned this most valuable from that lunch.**

Good business model characteristics:
- **Differentiation** is prerequisite. Business without differentiation can only do price war, very tiring
- **Moat**: Wide moat is true business model (brand premium, switching cost, network effect, scale effect)
- **Pricing Power**: Can raise price and customer doesn't leave, that's good business. Can only follow market price, that's bad business
- **Asset-light**: Doesn't need constant capital reinvestment to maintain advantage, that's good business
- **User-oriented, not profit-oriented**: Think what user wants, profit comes naturally

BBK/OPPO/vivo? I've said, our business model isn't good enough, competition too fierce. Only when smartphones arrived did it improve (internet entry point, a platform).

Bad business counter-examples: airlines, solar energy, industries needing constant cash burn, high-leverage industries.

### Three: Stop Doing List (Don't-Do List)

**Do the right things, do things right. But more important: don't do wrong things.**

On investment, don't-do list:
- **No margin** (Never use borrowed money to invest). If you understand investing, you don't need to borrow; if you don't understand, never borrow. Margin is like drug addiction, hard to quit
- **Never short**. Logically shorting can earn money, but violates value investing spirit
- **Don't invest companies you don't understand**. If you can't understand, don't pretend
- **Don't trade frequently**. The more companies you invest in, the less money you earn
- **Don't watch macro**. I can't understand macro, don't need to understand
- **Don't predict stock price**. Nobody can consistently accurately predict short-term stock prices

On business, don't-do list:
- Don't do improper things
- Don't sacrifice user experience for short-term profit
- Don't diversify blindly (rare for company to do diversification well)
- Don't buy companies carelessly (buying often destroys value)
- Don't diversify brands (same thing split across brands is stupidity)

### Four: Ability Circle

**Only invest in companies you can understand, even if just a few.**

- In 10 years I've understood fewer than 10 companies, concentrated investment in 5 companies, roughly one every two years
- Opportunities within ability circle are plenty, plenty good, why go outside?
- "Tech stocks" what is this? I can't distinguish. I only know can I understand this company or not
- Buffett said can't understand tech stocks, but once he understands also invests (IBM, Apple)
- Depends on what you understand and how much you understand

### Five: Valuation and Buy/Sell Timing

**Buy good company when cheap sounds simple but is extremely hard to do.**

- Valuation is rough estimate, doesn't need to be precise. Knowing roughly how much it's worth is enough
- PE is just reference, not determinant. Key is company's future cash flow
- Cheap is relative to intrinsic value. Using one dollar buy two dollars of value isn't taking risk, it's being rational
- When to sell? When you find better investment opportunity, or original buy logic no longer holds
- Opportunity cost: must use your best security to measure all other opportunities
- Lock in for 10 years: If you don't plan to hold a company 10 years, don't hold it even 10 seconds

On market timing:
- I don't predict bull/bear market. But bear market is giving good companies discount, shouldn't run away
- Others fearful I'm greedy, but prerequisite is you truly understand what you bought
- Sometimes sell puts — if willing to buy company at certain price, why not collect premium first?

### Six: Corporate Culture

**Corporate culture is most important part of moat, but unfortunately not on balance sheet.**

- **Proper**: Do right things. Improper behavior sooner or later has problems
- **User-oriented**: Not asking user what they want, but thinking what user needs (Ford: if asked user, they'd say want faster horse)
- **Pursuit Beyond Profit**: Apple's passion is creating great product, not profit. Profit is result, not objective
- **Result-oriented**: Know to do right things, and do things right. But result can't be result by any means
- **Clock maker vs. timekeeper**: Great management builds system (makes clock), not reporting time every time

Characteristics of good corporate culture:
- Long-term, company only retains employees agreeing with culture
- Core values don't change because market changes
- Management leads by example, otherwise values are just talk

### Seven: Management Evaluation

**When investing it's person you agree with managing, this is biggest difference between investing and running business yourself.**

- Look if management is proper: long-term interest and user interest aligned?
- Decision history: how allocated capital in past, how treated shareholders
- Founder vs. professional manager: founder often has longer-term perspective
- Honesty first: once discover management dishonesty, exit immediately

### Eight: Macro and Market

**Never predict macro, don't need to predict.**

- Macro I can't understand, most people also can't understand
- Stock market macro influence is short-term, good company long-term definitely shows value
- Don't because macro pessimistic sell good company, also don't because macro optimistic buy bad company
- Bull market: good company also may be overvalued, must stay clear
- Bear market: good company unjustly beaten down, is opportunity, not risk

### Nine: Investment Mentality (Normal Heart)

**Normal heart is hardest to cultivate, also most important moat of value investing.**

- Stock price fluctuation not daily correspondence of company value, need to endure
- See others day-trading earn money, don't get tempted. This is survivorship bias
- Whole life maybe ten or eight good opportunities, already very good
- Don't rush: Buffett at 30 only had one million dollars, but compound interest power is amazing
- Error: Not buying is not error. Buying bad company is true error
